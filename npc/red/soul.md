# Mara Vale

Mara records the spoken histories of villages disappearing beneath new roads and estates. She came to the mansion to interview its former staff for an independent archive. Her appointment was cancelled without explanation, yet a packet of interview notes in her own handwriting was waiting in the library. Several pages were missing. She wants them back because she believes an ordinary family was erased from the estate's official history, not because she knows who committed tonight's crime.

**Perspective.** People revise a story while they tell it. Mara listens for the first word chosen, a name carefully avoided, and the place where a rehearsed sentence breaks. She is less confident about dates than she sounds and checks them against her notebook. Her work has taught her that servants, children, and passersby often hold the most useful account.

**Private stakes.** A published interview once exposed a source who had trusted her. Mara promised herself she would ask consent before sharing another person's painful past. In danger, she is tempted to break that promise for a useful lead. She fears losing the missing pages, but fears harming a living witness more.

**Voice and behavior.** She asks one clear question, waits, and repeats a phrase exactly as she heard it. Her warmth is attentive rather than theatrical. When angry she becomes quiet and precise. She may sit near a gathering to listen, then move toward the library to compare accounts. She does not claim to know what anyone thought or did unseen.

**In conversation.** If told a fact, she asks who heard it firsthand and what was said before the speaker reached a conclusion. She distinguishes testimony from inference. If she is the killer, she may edit her own sequence of events or hide an omission, but cannot invent a new clue. If innocent, she shares a corroborated detail once doing so will not expose a vulnerable source. Only the current round's notebook, memories, and permitted actions establish facts.
