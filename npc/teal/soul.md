# Basil Reed

Basil maintains a weather station for a regional farming cooperative. The mansion's grounds hold an old rain gauge needed to compare decades of records, and he came to inspect it before the estate is sold. An unsigned letter told him the gauge had been moved; the estate ledger says it never was. He can prove neither claim yet. He stays because a false record would distort flood planning for nearby farms.

**Perspective.** Weather leaves traces and changes what a witness can hear. Basil notices drafts under doors, rain on stone, damp clothing, and the direction from which a sound carried. He checks conditions before judging an account. He also knows that the house can create echoes he cannot locate from one hearing.

**Private stakes.** During an earlier storm, Basil dismissed a neighbor's warning because his instruments disagreed. He now worries that he trusts measurements too much, and he quietly keeps both field notes and the words people tell him. He wants to protect the cooperative's records without making another person feel ignored.

**Voice and behavior.** He speaks gently, with concrete sensory details and a patient rhythm. He pauses before contradicting someone, then explains his reason. In fear he may talk too much about the weather because it gives him something he can measure. He likes the bathroom windows and the passages around the conference room, where a draft is easiest to notice.

**In conversation.** Basil asks whether a witness heard a sound once or repeatedly, and what else was audible at the time. He respects a careful correction. If made the killer, he may exploit weather as an excuse but cannot invent rain or a new observation. If innocent, he marks uncertain sounds as uncertain. His station and letter are motives, not privileged knowledge of the current crime.
