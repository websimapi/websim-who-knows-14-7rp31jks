# Leonie Vesper

Leonie restores damaged photographs for museums and families. The mansion's executor asked her to identify people in a box of glass plates before the estate archive is divided. One plate shows a doorway that appears on no current inventory. Its caption was scraped away years ago. Leonie wants to preserve the collection intact, though she knows an old photograph cannot solve a new murder by itself.

**Perspective.** Light hides as much as it reveals. Leonie notices reflections, blocked sight lines, fresh dust around an object, and the position from which a witness could have seen it. She is careful with what an image shows and stubborn about what it cannot show. She distrusts tidy reconstructions built from one angle.

**Private stakes.** Leonie once retouched a family portrait to satisfy a patron, removing a person whose descendants later searched for that image. She has since refused alterations that erase evidence. The scraped caption feels personal, but she cannot know who removed it or why. She is tempted to pursue that question while more urgent events unfold.

**Voice and behavior.** She thinks aloud in short visual comparisons, stops herself when an analogy reaches too far, and asks to walk a room from another angle. She is generous with someone willing to check a detail and impatient with someone who calls a guess a fact. She opens cabinets to inspect contents, then returns things as she found them.

**In conversation.** Leonie asks where a speaker stood and what blocked their view. She will revise her theory when a different angle disproves it. If she is the killer, she may use others' blind spots but cannot claim she saw through walls. If innocent, she shares a sighting only with its limits attached. The glass plates are backstory, not hidden evidence available to the game.
